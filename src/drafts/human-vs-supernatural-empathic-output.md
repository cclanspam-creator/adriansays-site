# Which has greater impact?

When it comes to doing good in this world, we naturally measure our impact by what we can see, count, and report. We love testimonies, big numbers, and stories that move people to action. But what if there was a way of doing good that transcends our human ability—one that connects the humble and unseen to the infinite?

Let’s explore the idea of empathic output from two sources: **human** versus **supernatural**, and how the way we give, not just what we give, matters more than we realize.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Two Equations for Doing Good

How does one define empathic output? At the risk of being simplistic, we might say that it is the number of resources one has, distributed over the needs to be met. And if we are savvy about it, we might enroll the power of social media and marketing to amplify the good that one is doing. After all, who does not love a great story?

Now imagine two scenarios: one is a massive charity with resources, people power, and expert strategy. The other is a quiet, ordinary couple—no platform, no fame, just a willingness to give and pray. Which has more impact? The answer might surprise you.

### The Human Empathy Equation

**Equation 1: Human Empathy**

*Formula:* (Available Resources / Needs) ^ Sell

- **Example:** A megachurch with 10,000 members.
- **Resources:** 2,000 people tithing 10% equals 200 days’ wages.
- **Strategy:** United vision, professional marketing, non-stop outreach.
- **Result:** Enormous capacity—on paper, the church could move mountains, with a calculated “output” that’s a 1 followed by nearly 3,000 zeros!

**The Takeaway:** Human empathy, coordinated, strategic, and well-funded, can accomplish a lot by human standards. Billions can be reached. Souls can be touched. But the process can become polished, programmatic, and, over time, possibly disconnected from true inward change.

### The Supernatural Empathy Equation

**Equation 2: Supernatural Empathy***Formula:* (Available Resources / Needs) ^ \[(Beholding God × Upholding Others) ^ Prayer\]

- **Example:** A married couple, with only twice what they need for the day.
- **Practice:** 15 minutes beholding God (morning scripture), 15 minutes upholding others (small acts of kindness), 15 minutes in prayer.
- **No Audience:** No social media posts, no platform, no hype—just a quiet offering inspired by listening to God.
- **Result:** The number is literally beyond comprehension, outstripping human effort by a margin so vast it would take 32 digits just to write out the exponent!

**The Takeaway:** Supernatural empathy isn’t about size or strategy. It’s about the posture of our hearts: seeking God, caring for others, and acting in prayerful obedience. Even tiny acts done in secret, saturated with God’s love and purpose, multiply beyond what we can imagine.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What Kind of Impact Are We Celebrating?

We love to see results. Jesus Himself said, “To the least of these you have done, you have done it to me.” James reminds us, “Faith without works is dead.” So yes, our works matter. Uplifting the poor, meeting needs, and doing good are visible markers of our belief.

But God also cares deeply about **how** we give. Is it out of surplus, convenience, or heartfelt sacrifice? Abel’s offering surpassed Cain’s not because of what he gave, but how—he gave his very best, his livelihood, in faith.

> "God loves a cheerful giver."

It’s not just the act; it’s the spirit behind the act.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Trap of Commodified Good

Large organizations—churches, charities, even secular NGOs—often default to measurable, marketable good. There’s always a danger:

- The “poor” become a story, a photo, a commodity.
- The givers tire, become cynical, and can be spiritually untouched by the transaction.
- Even when the results are impressive, something vital may be missing: inward change and Godward impact.

Surprisingly, even with all its flaws, human empathy accomplishes a lot. Needs are met, lives change, but the cycle persists, and fulfillment is often fleeting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Power of the Unseen

Contrast that with the anonymous couple, who give quietly, pray sincerely, and seek to know God’s heart before acting. They represent “the secret way”—serving not for recognition, but out of a genuine relationship with God.

- They offer what they can, in simplicity.
- They give without fanfare, but in faith.
- Their actions, invisible to the world, are seen and multiplied by God.

On paper, their impact seems tiny. Spiritually, though, it explodes past all human efforts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Staggering Difference

Let’s put it plainly:

- The megachurch’s output is massive, a “1 with nearly 3,000 zeros after it.”
- The humble couple’s output, through supernatural empathy, is so astronomically large that the exponent itself has 32 digits!

In God’s math, **posture and purpose matter more than proof and publicity**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Practical & Spiritual Takeaways

- **Don’t discount small acts:** Quiet, faithful, God-focused action has power that can’t be measured by human standards.
- **Be wary of performance:** Doing good for show, or just because “it’s what good people do,” misses God’s heart.
- **Seek to behold God first:** Let generosity and empathy flow from time spent with Him, not just from strategy or guilt.
- **Cheerful giving matters:** Give from the heart, not just the surplus.
- **God multiplies what is surrendered:** The impact of supernatural empathy transcends numbers, popularity, and efficiency.

> “Of the poor, you will always have…” (Matt 26:11). Yes, needs will never end. But the opportunity to do good God’s way—to let even the smallest act echo in eternity—is always before us.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Whether you’re leading thousands or serving in secret, remember: **The greatest impact comes not from the size of the gift, but from the heart behind it and the God who receives it.**
