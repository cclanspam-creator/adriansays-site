So I've been working on a project to help see how fathers develop their fathering identities. I wish I can say that I have discovered 10 tips for amazing fathering, or that I have managed to derive the most impactful fathering approach. I mean, sure, I am collating the tips and suggestions. But emerging from the data is also a different narrative, an almost confessional one. What do I mean?

## The Fathering-Flourishing Gap

A very common soundbite I have heard from the interviews goes something like this," I am an ongoing work in progress/I've made so many mistakes as a father/I don't know what I did as a father, but I am so glad my kids turned out okay/great!"

Many of the fathers seem to allude that they have not much to do with their kids' flourishing. Maybe they are humble and highly aware of their own frail humanity. Or it could be that they are enlightened for having realised that control over the lives of others is largely a self-made delusion. Whatever the reason is, I find in the data a certain distancing between a father's effort and a child's flourishing. Which is counter-intuitive to me.

## Impactful Fathering: Just not how one expects it

In my mind, I believe there are things fathers do that should make an impact. Things such as being present and involved, teaching, supporting, role modeling various desirable attributes, etc. Thankfully, the data so far suggests these relationships as well. The data supports that there is good that fathers do, so do not discount the role of fathers!

However, the data also seems to suggest that there is a big gap between intention and reception, between impartation and internalisation. Put simply, fathers may have good intentions, but more often than not, the kids take to heart the darnest things. They learn what fathers never intend to teach, and they pick up what fathers unconsciously transmit.

I have been asking myself, "Surely intentionality is important? Surely for the things that fathers have been really intentional about, there is some success?" In the greater scheme of things, it's still a hit and miss. In fact, it is more miss than hit. What fathers intend, they may not express or execute effectively. And even when fathers execute their intentions well, it may not be what the kids imbibe. The final outcome between fathering and flourishing is therefore less causal than we think.

I will continue to delve into the research data. But this is definitely food for thought. I'd love to hear your views as well.

## From Intentionality of Fathers to Agency in Children

In the meantime, I recall stories of kids who turn out alright in spite of blundering fathers. Or of kids with adverse childhood experiences who rise above their upbringing. I am sure you have your own stories to share as well.

Hence, while fathering is important, there is another dynamic at play, which is the child's response to adversity, to material blessing, to crisis, and of course, to competent fathering. It appears there is much that a child can do to take ownership over one's own development. This is such an important factor that I should look into this more specifically in future.

In addition, my research did not go into the significant roles that mothers and surrogate mothers play, or the positive impact of responsible siblings - these effects are already well-documented in the literature, so it is not the focus of my research in this project. Nonetheless, it goes to show that there is a constellation of factors at play, such that it would be foolhardy to ignore them, even though the object of my research is on fathering.

## From Agency to Honouring

Finally, I also came across a passage in my personal reflections that gave me something else to mull over. Yesterday, I read in Ps 112: “Happy is the man who fears the LORD, taking great delight in His commands. His descendants will be powerful in the land;”

This verse inserts God between fathering and flourishing. It suggests that God the Heavenly Father will personally father us and our children. He will bless our best endeavours, and He will rescue us from our blunders. Above all, He is pleased with the one who has utmost regard for Him, to the point that He rewards the next generation with great blessing.

I wonder how this informs my research. It brings me back from a focus on a child's agency to an attitude of honouring in the father. In a way, it is more in line wiht the focus of my research, which is about the fathering identity, but it also introduces faith into the equation. Certainly, the fathering journey is arduous enough to demand a measure of faith. And indeed, many of the fathers interviewed demonstrated faith even when the going gets tough. It is such an intriguing proposition! Fear the Lord and delight in His commands. Get this step right, and everything else falls into place, in spite of ourselves, and beyond our best selves. The best fathering a father can offer is simply to be a faithful son himself!
