Being one…This is something we hear every now and then. Lovers crave for it (I want to be one with you!). Spiritualists work towards it (to be one with the universe). Leaders demand it (we need to be united). But what exactly does it mean to be one with anything or anyone?

## Different Types of Oneness

### 1. Organisational Oneness

Let's start with a common organizational need described in various ways such as esprit de corp, organisational identity, culture, or DNA. The need for a sense of belonging in "We are family", "We are one tribe", essentially describe similar notions.

Strong work culture cultivates oneness in organizations. Strong relationships at work (i.e., relational oneness) also help build organizational oneness. Finally, shared goals and a sense of common destiny does wonders to gel people together, regardless of their ethnicity, nationality. These three work together like the different legs of a stool to help keep an organization on even keel.

It is easy for leaders to settle for outward homogeniety rather than to forge oneness. Just because people wear the same uniform to work does not imply they are cut from the same cloth. And just because people look different does not mean they are not in alignment in their hearts and minds.

### 2. Relational Oneness

This is most common in relationships. Much of the coaching work I do in organizations stem from people bolted to the same team but not being on the same page. Sometimes, people bring their "less-than-ideal" selves to work because of a lack of relational oneness in their marriages or families.

To be one in this sense is to have relational harmony. It is to be at peace with each other, to see each other's perspectives without needing to be iron-caged by them emotionally, and to work well leveraging on each other's strengths to cover for each others' weaknesses.

### 3. Organizational Oneness and Optics

A related idea here is the much abused word that such leaders fall back to - that of "optics". "*We need to keep the optics right,*" they would say. "*We must not have a black, female, short, white, male, tall, rich, poor, or \<insert your personal descriptor\> leader here, because this person does not project the right optics.*" It is as if one is purchasing furniture and that all one expects of the leader is to perform flowerpot duties.

Leaders need to look beyond surface traits. This is what it means to democratize talent. Leaders need to recognize talent, no matter the outward wrappings. Organizational oneness based purely on relationships and not on merit-based talent deployment is in danger of becoming nothing more than a fraternity club.

### 3. Personal Oneness

the merging and melding of various identities. The submission of personal identity to the greater whole
PC: tie back to leadership of the self (self check and self refinement)

Transcendence ONE: Can disparate entities become a transcendent one? Gestalt oneness where the sum of its parts are greater than the whole

TRINITARIAN ONE: Can different entities be both unique and yet one? Holy Trinity is an example
One can be Buddhist, Bhutanese, and Budding entrepreneur. One can also be Christian, civil servant, and also committed.
