O-N-E: Being Church After 2020

In January 2021 I sat on a panel at the LoveSingapore Pastors' Summit with two pastors. We were asked what the Church had learnt from the pandemic, and what it should look like in the new normal. I was serving as Elder Chair at International Baptist Church at the time. Salt&Light reported the session.

This is a write-up of what I shared that day, so that it can sit alongside what I wrote in April 2020 on being a FACTER for good.

What we had to figure out together

When services went online, something became obvious. You cannot watch prayer on a screen. You cannot watch worship either, however good the musicians are. You have to take part.

So in every household that turned on the livestream, someone had to lead the worship, someone had to pray and someone had to take charge. Not many of us were equipped for that. Perhaps we leaders had made ourselves such critical providers that we stood in the way of God equipping His people.

Evangelism raised a similar question. In my church it usually happened within ministries. But how do you rally people around a ministry under safe distancing? I said then, "Maybe we have to move from ministries to movements." We go where the Spirit of God is moving. There is much to unlearn before we climb out of the valley, and the low point is part of the learning.

O-N-E

When we were each asked for one word to describe the Church going forward, I offered ONE, and spelt it out.

O is for Oikos. Go back to the family. Each household is a church in small. What we learn about being church at home, we carry into being family in church.

N is for Nothing unto ourselves. We must decrease so that God can increase. The pandemic showed how quickly our plans can fail, and how even giants can fall.

E is for Ecclesia. When the first two are in place, the Church can emerge. There is only one unity in Christ. There can be many organisations, but we are to be united for the sake of the ecclesia.

Nine months on from FACTER

FACTER was about how to do good together when we could not gather. O-N-E was my attempt, nine months later, to say what that season was asking of the Church. My closing appeal to the pastors in the room was simply that we be one in Christ.
